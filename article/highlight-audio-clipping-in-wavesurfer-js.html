Highlight Audio Clipping in Wavesurfer.js

Visualizing audio clipping in Wavesurfer.js helps audio engineers and listeners immediately spot distorted dynamic peaks. This article covers specific color customization methods—including canvas vertical gradients, high-contrast alerting palettes, and plugin overlays—that make clipped waveforms visually prominent against normal dynamic ranges.

Vertical Canvas Gradients for Peak Thresholds

Wavesurfer.js accepts a native HTML5 CanvasGradient for both waveColor and progressColor. Using a vertical linear gradient is the most effective native method to emphasize peaks nearing 0 dBFS (the top and bottom edges of the canvas).

To highlight clipping:

  • Create a canvas context gradient spanning from the top (y = 0) to the bottom (y = height) of the waveform canvas.
  • Assign high-alert colors (such as #FF0033 or #FF3B30) at the extreme stops (0.0 and 1.0), which represent peak positive and negative amplitudes.
  • Transition sharply into safe colors (such as cool blues, slate, or neutral grays) around the threshold where clipping begins (typically normalized between 0.1–0.2 from the edges).
  • Keep the center region (representing low-amplitude audio) subtle and desaturated to create contrast against the alert-colored tips.

High-Contrast Color Combinations

Effective visual alerts depend on luminance and saturation differences between safe audio and clipped audio:

  • Dark Mode Waveforms: Use a deep charcoal (#1E1E24) or dark slate base for standard signal levels, paired with saturated neon red (#FF1744), hot magenta (#FF007F), or vivid amber (#FF9100) at the clipping boundaries.
  • Light Mode Waveforms: Pair a mid-tone cool gray (#6C757D) or muted navy (#2B4C7E) base with an aggressive crimson (#D90429).
  • Monochrome Baselines: Using monochromatic, low-saturation tones for safe dynamics ensures that bright alert tones immediately draw the eye without competing visual noise.

Synchronizing Progress and Played States

Clipped points should remain visible even after playback passes over them. To preserve visibility across both states:

  • Match the gradient stop positions across both waveColor and progressColor.
  • Maintain the clipping color (such as bright red) at the edges in both states.
  • Vary only the body of the waveform (e.g., changing an unplayed dark gray body into a vivid cyan or white body upon playback) while leaving the clipping tips consistently highlighted.

Adding Visual Regions for Clipped Sections

When vertical gradient coloration is not distinct enough for brief transient clips, the Wavesurfer.js Regions plugin can highlight time-based intervals where clipping occurs.

  • Analyze peak data before or during load to locate sample values hitting 1.0 or -1.0.
  • Insert micro-regions at those exact timestamps using a semi-transparent red background (e.g., rgba(255, 0, 0, 0.35)) and a solid red border.
  • This technique colors the entire vertical slice of the timeline, ensuring that even single-sample clips that produce narrow visual spikes cannot be overlooked.
