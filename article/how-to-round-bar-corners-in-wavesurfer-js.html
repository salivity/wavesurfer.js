How to Round Bar Corners in Wavesurfer.js

Customizing the visual appearance of audio waveforms is a common requirement when building interactive media players, and Wavesurfer.js makes this straightforward. This guide explains which configuration property controls the rounded corners of waveform bars, how to implement it, and how it interacts with other styling settings to create modern, polished audio visualizations.

In Wavesurfer.js, the option that controls the rounded corners of waveform bars is barRadius. This property accepts an integer or floating-point number representing the radius in pixels applied to the ends of each bar in the waveform canvas.

To use barRadius, pass it into the configuration object when initializing your Wavesurfer instance using WaveSurfer.create():

const wavesurfer = WaveSurfer.create({
  container: '#waveform',
  waveColor: '#4F46E5',
  progressColor: '#818CF8',
  barWidth: 3,
  barGap: 2,
  barRadius: 3,
  url: '/path/to/audio.mp3',
});

For barRadius to take effect, the waveform must be rendered as bars rather than a continuous line. This means you must also define a barWidth greater than zero. If barWidth is omitted, Wavesurfer.js defaults to rendering a continuous path where corner radius settings do not apply.

Setting barRadius equal to or greater than half of the barWidth will produce fully rounded, pill-shaped bars. For subtler rounding, choose a value smaller than half of the barWidth. Combining barRadius with barWidth and barGap gives you full control over the spacing, thickness, and overall aesthetic of your audio visualization.
