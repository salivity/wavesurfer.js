How autoCenter Affects Seeking in Wavesurfer.js

The autoCenter configuration option in Wavesurfer.js determines whether the waveform's viewport automatically shifts to keep the playhead centered on the screen during playback and user interactions. When working with scrollable, zoomed-in waveforms, configuring this property fundamentally alters the visual seeking behavior, dictating whether clicking to seek results in an immediate viewport repositioning or keeps the current view stationary.

In Wavesurfer.js, waveforms become scrollable when audio is zoomed in using settings like minPxPerSec. Under these conditions, the visible container only displays a portion of the entire audio timeline. The autoCenter boolean option (which is enabled by default in many versions) controls how the library manages the horizontal scroll offset whenever the playback position changes.

Seeking with autoCenter: true

When autoCenter is set to true, seeking anywhere on the waveform causes the container to immediately scroll horizontally so that the target seek point is aligned with the exact center of the visible area.

This behavior applies to both user-driven seeking (clicking or dragging across the waveform) and programmatic seeking (calling methods like seekTo() or setTime()). While this guarantees that the user always has an equal view of the audio immediately preceding and following the playhead, it can cause an abrupt visual jump. Clicking near the edge of the visible waveform will instantly re-center that point, moving the surrounding audio context out from under the user's cursor.

Seeking with autoCenter: false

Setting autoCenter to false disables the automatic centering mechanism during seek events. When a user clicks to seek within the currently visible viewport, the playhead moves directly to the clicked position, but the waveform container does not scroll to center it.

This provides a more stable, non-disruptive editing experience. Users can inspect, scrub, and click through a specific section of the audio without the waveform continuously jumping beneath their cursor. If combined with standard scrolling behavior, the viewport will only scroll when the playhead naturally advances past the edge of the screen, rather than forcing a center alignment on every click.

Choosing the Right Behavior

  • Enable autoCenter (true): Best suited for standard audio players, transcription players, or continuous playback scenarios where the primary objective is maintaining equal context around the current playback position at all times.
  • Disable autoCenter (false): Ideal for audio editors, annotation tools, and detailed waveform manipulation where sudden viewport shifts disrupt precision clicking and region selection.
