seekTo vs seekAndCenter in Wavesurfer.js

This article explains the difference between the seekTo and seekAndCenter methods in the Wavesurfer.js audio visualization library. While both methods change the current playback position of an audio track, they differ significantly in how they handle viewport scrolling and visual presentation when dealing with zoomable, scrollable waveforms.

The Core Difference

The fundamental difference lies in viewport alignment:

  • seekTo(progress) moves the playback position to a specified progress point (a float between 0 and 1) without explicitly forcing the visible waveform to center around the playhead.
  • seekAndCenter(progress) moves the playback position to a specified progress point and immediately scrolls the waveform container horizontally so that the playhead is positioned directly in the center of the visible viewport.

Understanding seekTo

When you call seekTo(progress), Wavesurfer updates the internal audio playback position and renders the playhead at the corresponding location along the waveform.

If the waveform fits entirely inside its container without horizontal scrolling (e.g., when minPxPerSec is low or autoCenter is disabled), seekTo simply shifts the cursor line. If the waveform is long, zoomed in, and requires horizontal scrolling, calling seekTo updates the playhead position, but it will not guarantee that the playhead remains visible if it falls outside the currently scrolled view.

// Moves playhead to the 50% mark of the audio
wavesurfer.seekTo(0.5);

Understanding seekAndCenter

The seekAndCenter(progress) method is designed specifically for scrollable waveform implementations. When an audio file is zoomed in using a high minPxPerSec value, the full waveform often extends far beyond the width of the visible container.

Calling seekAndCenter(progress) executes two actions simultaneously:

  1. It navigates to the target playback timestamp.
  2. It calculates the horizontal scroll offset of the container and scrolls it so the playhead is placed dead center on the user's screen.
// Moves playhead to the 25% mark and centers the view on it
wavesurfer.seekAndCenter(0.25);

When to Use Each Method

  • Use seekTo when building custom scrubbing controls, timeline sliders, or mini-maps where you want direct manual control over the scroll position, or when the entire waveform is always visible within the screen width.
  • Use seekAndCenter when navigating between specific markers, regions, or chapters in a zoomed-in waveform, ensuring the user immediately sees the visual context of the audio segment without having to manually scroll horizontally to find the playhead.
