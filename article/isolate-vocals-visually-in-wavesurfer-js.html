Isolate Vocals Visually in Wavesurfer.js

Isolating vocalisations visually in Wavesurfer.js requires moving beyond standard time-domain amplitude waveforms to frequency-based visualization and targeted audio filtering. Because standard waveforms only plot raw volume over time, voice components often blend invisibly with instruments and background noise. Developers achieve clear visual vocal isolation primarily by integrating the Wavesurfer.js Spectrogram plugin, applying bandpass pre-filtering via the Web Audio API, or highlighting vocal boundaries using the Regions plugin alongside Voice Activity Detection (VAD).

1. Utilizing the Spectrogram Plugin

The primary technique for visually isolating vocalisations is replacing or augmenting the default waveform with a spectrogram via the official @wavesurfer/spectrogram plugin.

Human speech possesses distinct harmonic signatures known as formants, typically concentrated between 300 Hz and 3,400 Hz (with fundamental frequencies between 85 Hz and 255 Hz). While a waveform flattens these frequencies into generic peaks, a spectrogram displays time on the horizontal axis, frequency on the vertical axis, and intensity through color.

To isolate speech visually:

  • Set an FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) size between 512 and 2048 to achieve the optimal balance between time and frequency resolution.
  • Adjust the frequency scale to focus on the human vocal range (0 Hz to roughly 4,000–5,000 Hz) to eliminate non-vocal low-end rumble and high-end percussion.
  • Configure custom color maps to make the energy clusters corresponding to vowel formants and consonant bursts stand out clearly against background audio.

2. Bandpass Pre-Filtering the Audio Buffer

If an amplitude-style waveform is preferred over a spectrogram, vocalisations can be isolated by pre-filtering the audio data before rendering.

Using the browser's native OfflineAudioContext, the audio file can be processed through a bandpass filter (or combined highpass and lowpass BiquadFilterNode filters) calibrated specifically to the vocal range (e.g., passing 300 Hz to 3,000 Hz) prior to decoding. When the resulting audio buffer is loaded into Wavesurfer.js, the waveform amplitude directly reflects vocal energy rather than bass lines or high-frequency instruments, effectively isolating the presence of the human voice.

3. Combining Voice Activity Detection with the Regions Plugin

To clearly delineate vocal boundaries on top of a rendered waveform, developers use the Regions plugin (@wavesurfer/regions) paired with Voice Activity Detection (VAD).

By analyzing the audio using a client-side or server-side VAD algorithm (such as Silero VAD or WebRTC VAD), exact start and end timestamps of speech segments are identified. These timestamps are then passed into Wavesurfer's Regions plugin, which programmatically renders colored overlays across the waveform to highlight precisely where vocal tracks appear.
