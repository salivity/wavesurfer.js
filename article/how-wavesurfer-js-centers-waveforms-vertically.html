How Wavesurfer.js Centers Waveforms Vertically

This article explains the internal mechanics of vertical waveform alignment in Wavesurfer.js. By understanding how the library translates raw audio buffer data into pixel coordinates on an HTML5 <canvas>, developers can better manipulate rendering behaviors, customize visual appearances, and troubleshoot alignment issues across mono and multi-channel audio tracks.

The Canvas Coordinate Baseline

Wavesurfer.js renders visual audio representations using HTML5 <canvas> elements. In standard canvas rendering, the coordinate origin (0, 0) is positioned at the top-left corner, with the Y-axis increasing downward.

To center a waveform vertically, Wavesurfer.js establishes an artificial baseline at half the total canvas height:

\[\text{Center Y} = \frac{\text{Canvas Height}}{2}\]

This mid-point represents silence (a zero-amplitude state) in standard audio signals.

Peak Normalization and Amplitude Mapping

During audio decoding, Wavesurfer extracts channel data consisting of floating-point values typically ranging between -1.0 and 1.0. These values are extracted into an array of min/max peak pairs:

  1. Min/Max Pairs: For each horizontal pixel column (or bar), the library calculates the maximum positive peak and the minimum negative peak within that slice of time.
  2. Scaling to Height: The peak values are multiplied by half of the canvas height. A maximum peak of 1.0 extends to the very top edge (Y = 0), and a minimum peak of -1.0 extends to the very bottom edge (Y = Canvas Height).
  3. Drawing the Slice: For each vertical slice, the engine draws a line from (Center Y - positivePeak * halfHeight) down to (Center Y - negativePeak * halfHeight). Because the positive and negative limits scale equally outward from the center baseline, the waveform remains centered.

Bar Alignment Options

In modern versions of Wavesurfer.js, the vertical centering behavior can be explicitly configured via rendering options.

  • Default (Centered): Omitting alignment settings or setting barAlign to center ensures that peaks extend equally upward and downward from the middle of the track.
  • Top Alignment (barAlign: 'top'): The baseline shifts to Y = 0. The visual waveform hangs downward from the top edge.
  • Bottom Alignment (barAlign: 'bottom'): The baseline shifts to Y = Canvas Height. Waveform peaks project upward from the bottom of the container.

Multi-Channel Audio and Centering

When handling stereo or multi-channel audio:

  • Merged View (Default): The library averages or composites the channels together and draws a single waveform centered along the main vertical axis.
  • Split Channels (splitChannels: true): Wavesurfer divides the container into distinct sub-canvases for each audio channel. Each channel is assigned its own dedicated canvas with its own relative center line at canvas.height / 2, ensuring every individual channel remains independently centered.
